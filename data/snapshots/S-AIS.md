# The use of AI in research creates many opportunities, and anxieties, for scholars. Broadly

# speaking, AIS recognizes opportunities available to scholars to responsibly use AI in their

# research. This includes, but is not limited to, using AI to brainstorm ideas, to discuss

perspectives on a theory, to get feedback on research design, and to improve the quality of writing. There are also irresponsible uses of AI in research such as training an AI using all the scholarly work by a specific other author and then asking the trained AI to rewrite one’s own work using the style of the other author, having AI write huge portions of a paper without

# acknowledgement, and incorporating references used by an AI without reading the papers or

# even confirming their existence. However, between these responsible uses that enhance research

# outcomes and irresponsible uses that threaten to undermine the very credibility of scientific

knowledge we seek to advance, there is a rather large grey area that creates significant ambiguity for scholars.

# In an effort to help authors navigate this uncertain terrain, we have prepared principles regarding

AI use in research and guidelines for responsible disclosure of AI use in research that we hope will (1) alleviate author concerns that if they disclose any AI use, their paper will be viewed as illegitimate, (2) encourage authors to ensure their own use of AI in research does not cross a line into irresponsible use, (3) serve as a dynamic guide that will be regularly revisited and updated

# as new AI forms and new AI uses emerge.

# Principles

# AIS adheres to the following principles regarding AI use in research:

Principle 1: Scholarship and science stand to benefit from the responsible use of AI in research.

# Principle 2: Scholars increasingly use AI to improve the efficiency and quality of their scholarly

# endeavors and should not be penalized for so doing; however, scholars must retain intellectual

ownership of their scholarship and assume accountability for their work, including any contributions aided or generated by AI models.

# Principle 3: Authors should ensure that the knowledge the paper reports is knowledge the authors

# themselves fully understand and are able to independently explain and justify. AI should only be

utilized in research activities where the authors possess the requisite expertise necessary to independently verify and defend the results.

# Principle 4: Authors should disclose which sections of their paper, and more generally which

activities in the conduct of their research, were influenced by the use of AI and the nature, form

# and extent that of such influence.

Principle 5: In cases where the AI itself generated original composition for a paper (as opposed to polishing the authors’ own writing), the authors need to disclose upon submission of the paper the specific AI and representative prompts that were used in the interaction with the AI and the

# specific sections of the paper that were originally composed by AI.

Principle 6: AI should never be the sole contributor to the originality of insight and knowledge

# in a paper. Substantive human intellectual contribution is required. In subsections of papers

where originality of insight and knowledge do not add to the paper’s contribution, AI use should

# not be restricted; however, AI-generated content must never bypass the authors' rigorous verification.

# Principle 7: Paper submissions to AIS journals and conferences should not be desk rejected

# based solely on the disclosure of AI use with the exception of:

# (a) cases where the authors have obviously neither composed nor verified the content, such as

# when there are many fabricated references or false claims attributed to real references;

(b) cases where authors have clearly used AI in some capacity but have not disclosed such use;
(c) cases where there is no indication of original human content in any section of the paper. The above principles are intended to support the responsible use of AI as an aid to, but not a substitute for, human intellectual contribution in scholarly work
# Responsible Disclosure Guidelines

# To assist authors with the uncertain areas concerning AI use in research, AIS supports the

# following guidelines regarding the responsible disclosure of AI in research. General disclosure

follows from Principle 4 and refers to an acknowledgement of the use of AI to assist the

# author(s) with various activities involved in the conducting of the research and the writing and

# preparation of the paper. Authors mention the AI used but not the details concerning prompts.

*Specific disclosure follows from Principle 5 and refers to an explicit statement of what AI was*

# used, what information was provided the AI, and representative prompts that were given to the

AI to achieve the output as well as how, if at all, the output was evaluated or altered by the

# authors prior to use in the research or paper.
