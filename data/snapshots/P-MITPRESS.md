# MIT Press Policy on the Use of Generative AI in Scholarly Publishing

The MIT Press recognizes that authors may use generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools and AI-assisted technologies in the development of scholarly works. These technologies can support activities such as language editing, summarization, translation, coding assistance, data analysis, image generation, and drafting of text. The use of such tools is permitted, subject to the requirements below. Disallowed usage includes but is not limited to generating citations, references, quotations, or source material without verifying their accuracy and existence, or creating or altering images, figures, or visual data in a manner that misrepresents research findings.

The disclosed, permissible use of generative AI tools will not be grounds for rejection. However, failure to disclose material use of generative AI in the preparation of a submission may result in rejection or retraction if such use comes to the attention of MIT Press during review, production, publication, or after publication.

## Human Authorship and Responsibility

Generative AI tools and systems cannot be listed as authors or co-authors of any MIT Press publication. Authorship carries responsibilities that include accountability for the accuracy, integrity, originality, and legality of a work. Because AI systems cannot assume these responsibilities, only human contributors may be identified as authors.

Authors remain fully responsible for all content in their submissions, including any content created, modified, analyzed, or assisted by AI tools.

## For Journal Article Authors

By submitting an article to an MIT Press journal, you are affirming that you are adhering to these guidelines and must disclose your use of generative AI or AI-assisted technologies at the time of submission per the Disclosure of AI Use section below. Upon acceptance of your article, the author agreement you sign will contain a section that requires you to disclose and describe AI usage in your article. You need not declare minimal, non-generative uses such as basic spelling and grammar correction or minor formatting. Anything involving data, analysis, or substantive content must be declared, however minor the contribution. This information will be replicated for readers in the final published article. Authors must retain full records of the methods undertaken, including full descriptions and records of the prompts used, which must be shared with the journal upon request. Individual journals may have additional AI disclosure requirements beyond what is represented here. Please be sure to check each journal’s submission guidelines for further information.

## For Book Authors

By submitting a proposal, book chapters, and/or manuscript to the MIT Press, you are affirming that you are adhering to these guidelines and must disclose your use of generative AI or AI-assisted technologies at the time of submission per the Disclosure of AI Use section below. Upon initial acceptance of the project and at draft or final submission of the manuscript, the Press will require that you disclose and describe AI usage. Published books will include disclosures of how AI was used in the creation and development of the Work.

## For Peer Reviewers

MIT Press manuscripts and associated review materials are confidential. Reviewers must not upload manuscripts, figures, data, reviewer reports, or editorial correspondence to public generative AI systems. Reviewers remain solely responsible for the content and conclusions of their reviews. Within a closed AI environment, reviewers may make limited use of AI-assisted tools for grammar, translation, accessibility, and statistical or analytical tasks such as checking reported computations, reproducing summary statistics, or assessing methodological claims. Such use is conditional on manuscript confidentiality being preserved in full, with no unpublished content disclosed to third parties or retained for model training. Responsibility for the accuracy of all output rests with the reviewer, and any substantive use must be declared. Reviewers who use ChatGPT or other generative AI tools to prepare review reports in a manner that violates this policy may have their reviews excluded from editorial consideration and may no longer be invited to review for MIT Press books or journals. Reviewers who have concerns about the inappropriate or undisclosed use of generative AI in a submission should bring those concerns to the attention of the journal or book editor.

## Disclosure of AI Use

Authors must disclose any material use of generative AI tools or large language models in the preparation of a submission, particularly where such use has contributed to the methodology, analysis, results, conclusions, references, text, images, or other substantive content. Authors should cite original sources rather than AI tools as primary references, except where citation of the AI tool is necessary to acknowledge its role in generating images, visualizations, or other content. Authors remain fully responsible for reviewing and verifying all AI-generated content, including factual claims, analyses, and citations, and for ensuring that all referenced sources exist and are accurately represented. All citations generated by a generative AI need to be manually checked for accuracy by the author(s) prior to submission.

Examples of material use include, but are not limited to:

- Drafting or generating text
- Revising or rewriting text beyond routine copyediting
- Generating figures, images, tables, or other visual content
- Generating or substantially modifying code
- Conducting analyses or interpreting data
- Summarizing literature or research findings
- Translating content

Routine uses that do not materially affect the intellectual content of a work, such as spell-checking, grammar correction, or formatting assistance, generally do not require disclosure.

Disclosures should identify:

1. The AI tool or system used including name and exact version;
2. The purpose for which it was used; and
3. The extent of its contribution to the work.

Disclosures should also confirm that the AI tool’s terms of use permit publication of the resulting content.

The MIT Press may publish AI disclosures alongside the final publication where appropriate.

## Use of Generative AI for Images and Figures

Generative AI may not be used to create, alter, manipulate, or enhance images, figures, or original research data in ways that affect the presentation or interpretation of scientific, scholarly, or clinical findings. This prohibition includes the addition, removal, modification, concealment, or relocation of features within research images, figures, or datasets.

Generative AI may be used to assist in the creation of research data visualizations, conceptual illustrations, process diagrams, educational figures, and other non-primary research images, provided such use is disclosed. Authors must identify the AI tool used, verify the accuracy and originality of the resulting content, ensure they have the necessary rights and permissions to publish it, and accept full responsibility for the integrity of the published work.

Where AI-assisted images are included in a submission, authors must disclose the use of the AI tool in the manuscript and identify the tool in the relevant figure legend. Authors should retain records of AI-generated image versions and prompts and provide them to the publisher upon request.

## Accuracy and Verification

Authors are responsible for reviewing and verifying all AI-generated content.

Authors must ensure that:

- Statements of fact are accurate;
- Citations and references are valid and accurately represented;
- Data, analyses, and conclusions are supported by appropriate evidence;
- AI-generated content does not contain plagiarism, fabricated information, or misleading representations.

The use of AI tools does not diminish an author’s responsibility and accountability for the integrity of the work.

## Editorial Review

The MIT Press may request additional information regarding disclosed AI use and may determine whether specific uses are appropriate for publication. The MIT Press reserves the right to require revision, clarification, additional disclosure, or removal of AI-generated content where necessary to maintain scholarly standards and research integrity.
