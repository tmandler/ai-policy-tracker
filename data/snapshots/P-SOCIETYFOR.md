# SIAM Publications – Editorial Policy on Artificial Intelligence

_Version 2.0 — Effective May 2026_

SIAM recognizes the rapidly evolving potential for AI tools to support and enhance our research, but such tools must never be used as a replacement for human judgment, expertise, or accountability.

The following policies therefore describe how SIAM authors, referees, and editors may use AI tools in a responsible, ethical, and transparent way.

## SIAM Authors

All submissions to SIAM should reflect human intention, expertise, and accountability. Given the well‑documented limitations of AI tools, all SIAM authors must adhere to the following policies.

1. Authors must be human and able to directly defend all aspects of the submission. In general, every co-author assumes accountability for the submission, revisions, and final accepted version. If any co-author should be excluded from such responsibility (as may be the case if a contributor is only involved with one narrow aspect of a submission), this must be explicitly noted in the footnote to the title and in the submission information.

2. Authors who use AI tools must do so responsibly. Usage of AI tools must be reported in either the “Acknowledgements” or “Declarations” section along with a statement confirming that “The authors assume responsibility for all content.” Areas for diligence and documentation include, but are not limited to, the following.



1. **Literature reviews and drafting related‑works sections.** AI-generated summaries of referenced papers may distort or fabricate the claims of the original work. Authors should exercise due diligence in ensuring that related-works sections are accurate and comprehensive.
2. **Formulating theorems, algorithms, proofs, and other claims**. AI tools may produce plausible looking but incorrect proofs, derivations, or algorithm statements that are difficult to check. Authors – not referees – bear responsibility for ensuring the correctness of all mathematical content.
3. **Writing code and conducting computational experiments.** Code produced by AI tools may contain errors, incorrectly implement a method, or silently fail on edge cases. Authors – not referees – bear responsibility for testing their codes. Preferably, all AI-assisted codes are made accessible (e.g., via a public repository) to enable reproducibility.
4. **Generating synthetic data or processing data.** When AI tools are used to generate data or process data, the methodology must be described in sufficient detail to assess validity and enable reproducibility, as with any data-generation method. Ideally, both the code and data should be made accessible (as with software).
5. **Producing figures or visualizations.** Technical figures produced directly by generative AI (rather than by code) may contain fabricated details or misleading representations. Authors should ensure all figures are produced by verifiable, reproducible processes. Authors must ensure that all figures are correct and any figures or visualizations produced or edited by AI tools do not violate any copyright or trademark.
6. **Preparing citations or bibliographic materials.** AI-generated references may be fabricated or may misrepresent the claims of the original work. Authors are expected to have read every work that they cite and are responsible for ensuring that references to cited works are accurate and appropriate, as well as comprehensive.
7. Generally, grammar checking need not be disclosed.


3. Negligent or undisclosed usage of AI, whether intentional or not, is grounds for an author integrity investigation. Potential consequences include rejection of the submission, limits on future submissions to SIAM publications, notification of the authors' institutions, and correction or retraction of an already published article.

## SIAM Referees and Editors

It is paramount that SIAM referee reports and editor decisions reflect human judgment, expertise, and accountability. Referees and editors are responsible for protecting the confidentiality of materials provided to them during the peer review process.

SIAM referees are not permitted to share papers under review with generative AI tools in any form — including uploading, copy-pasting, or otherwise inputting manuscript content — as this compromises the confidentiality, copyright, and data privacy of material provided by the author during the peer review process.  Referees must personally carry out the core refereeing tasks and must not present AI-generated material as their own expert opinion. Referees are expected to comprehensively evaluate the submission materials themselves and to fully understand and be able to directly defend all aspects of their reports, both positive and negative.

The same prohibition against sharing papers under review with generative AI tools applies to editors, with exceptions only if (a) the Vice President of Publications has given explicit permission for the editor to use an AI tool under specific circumstances, and (b) the corresponding author explicitly gives permission for their paper to be processed using AI. All editors must personally carry out the core editorial tasks. They are expected to directly examine each submitted manuscript to determine whether it merits full review. Editors must make their own determinations about the referees to invite. Likewise, editors must fully read and carefully consider all referee reports before reaching any decision. None of these core responsibilities may be delegated to an AI system.

Failure to adhere to these policies is grounds for an integrity investigation. Potential consequences include removal as an editor or referee, limits on future submissions to SIAM publications, and notification of the institution of the editor/referee.

Note: This policy will be kept under regular review and changed as necessary in light of further technological developments in this area.
