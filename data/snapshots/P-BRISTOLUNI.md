## Journal Author Publication Ethics Policy

**Authorship**

- All named co-authors must consent to publication and to being named as a co-author. Please consult our [authorship criteria guidelines](https://bristoluniversitypress.co.uk/journals/author-toolkit/author-criteria) when assigning authorship.

- Requests for changes to authorship of a submitted article must be directed to the journal editors. Requests will be handled in accordance with the [relevant COPE guidelines](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/handling-changes-authorship-lists), and only permitted where valid reasons are provided and all authors agree to the change.

- If an authorship dispute arises, the review or publication process can be stopped until the dispute is resolved. If the dispute arises after an article has already been published editors will correct the scientific record in line with [COPE guidelines](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/handling-changes-authorship-lists), through an erratum, corrigendum or, if necessary, an editorial expression of concern or retraction.

- All forms of authorship misconduct, including [‘ghost’, ‘guest’ or ‘gift’ authorship](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/flowchart/how-recognise-potential-authorship-problems) will be investigated in line with [COPE guidelines](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/handling-changes-authorship-lists). If such practices are identified editors will correct the scientific record and may choose to inform the authors’ institutions.

- We do not recognise AI as meeting the conditions of authorship and authors are expected to bear full responsibility for ensuring the originality and accuracy of their work. The use of both assistive and generative AI tools must be explicitly declared and detailed within submitted manuscripts, and we reserve the right to reject any submission that violates our policy. Journal Editors will use their judgement regarding acceptable use of AI on a case by case basis, and individual journals may have their own specific policies on AI. We understand that the practical implementation of AI is evolving rapidly, and our position is subject to periodic review. Authors are encouraged to refer to their editorial contact(s) should they have any queries about this policy. More information on Bristol University Press' AI policy can be found here [Bristol University Press \| Policy on authorship and AI](https://bristoluniversitypress.co.uk/author-hub/policy-on-authorship-and-ai).


**Article submission criteria**

- All authors must certify that the article is their own original work.

- Authors should only submit a manuscript to one journal at a time and must confirm at the time of submission that submitted articles or related articles have not been published elsewhere and that they are not under consideration for publication by other journals.

- All material drawn on must be referenced appropriately.

- Permission must be cleared to re-use any content that has not been created by the author(s). Authors should contact the journal editorial office with evidence of the granted permissions. Advice on when permission is needed and how to request permission to reproduce any content published by Bristol University Press and Policy Press can be found on our [Rights and Permissions](https://bristoluniversitypress.co.uk/rights-and-permissions) page.

- Authors should avoid making any statements about individuals or organisations that could be construed as libel, slander or defamation.


**Conflicts of interest**

The Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) defines conflicts of interest in its [Guidelines on Good Publication Practice](https://bristoluniversitypress.co.uk/sectionAdmin/block/https:/publicationethics.org/files/u7141/1999pdf13.pdf):

_“Conflicts of interest comprise those which may not be fully apparent and which may influence the judgment of author, reviewers, and editors. They have been described as those which, when revealed later, would make a reasonable reader feel misled or deceived. They may be personal, commercial, political, academic or financial. “Financial” interests may include employment, research funding, stock or share ownership, payment for lectures or travel, consultancies and company support for staff.”_

- Authors must declare potential conflicts of interest when prompted during the online submission process.

- It is the responsibility of the corresponding author to confirm whether co-authors have any potential conflicts of interest.

- They must also include a conflict of interest declaration at the end of their final published article, before the funding acknowledgement (if applicable).


**If no conflicts exist**, please state _“The Author(s) declare(s) that there is no conflict of interest”._

**If potential conflicts exist** please supply details. Suggested wording: _“I/we have read and understood the Bristol University Press/Policy Press guidelines for declaration of conflicts of interest and declare the following interests: …\[please specify\]”._

**Research ethics**

- Authors must follow national and international procedures that govern the ethics of experimentation on humans and animals as appropriate for their discipline.

- If authors include details, images; or videos relating to individual research participants in their manuscript, they must obtain written informed consent for participation and publication from the participants (or their parent or legal guardian).

- Authors must declare whether clearance was granted by their institution or an external ethics committee and provide a statement detailing the approval granted. If research involved human or animal participants but authors did not obtain formal ethics approval, they must include a statement to that effect providing the reason.


**Research misconduct**

Our journal editors will investigate allegations of misconduct in accordance with COPE guidelines, and we provide advice and support to them in investigating allegations of misconduct. Any suspected breaches of ethical behaviour relating to Bristol University Press or Policy Press journals should be reported to the journal editors and Edwina Thorn, the BUP Journals Manager ( [edwina.thorn@bristol.ac.uk](mailto:edwina.thorn@bristol.ac.uk)).

- Editors will handle any instances of redundant or duplicated publication in accordance with [COPE guidelines](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/handling-duplicated-or-redundant-content-salami-slicing). [COPE’s definition](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/handling-duplicated-or-redundant-content-salami-slicing) of redundant/duplicate publication is: ‘The publication, or attempted publication, of whole or substantial parts of work/data/analysis that have already been published (or have been submitted elsewhere), without transparency or appropriate declaration/referencing’.

- Articles are screened for plagiarism prior to acceptance using text-comparison software.

- Any instances of suspected plagiarism in a submitted or published article will be fully investigated in line with [COPE guidance](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/flowchart/plagiarism-submitted-manuscript) and appropriate action taken. This may include rejecting the article, publishing a correction or retraction, and/or informing the authors' institutions.

- The deliberate inclusion of any fabricated or falsified data is a serious ethical breach and will be treated as misconduct. Fabricated or falsified data can include survey data where values have been altered, composite interviews (unless clearly labelled as such), images which have been manipulated, for example, to change the context, data sets or images which have been generated by AI or other methods, omission of selected data points or participants. Any alterations of original source data should be clearly explained in the article. Authors should be prepared to supply original data files on request. If an article is found to include fabricated data appropriate action will be taken in line with [COPE guidance](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/flowchart/fabricated-data-submitted-manuscript). This may include contacting the authors’ institutions, regulatory bodies or research councils, rejecting the article, or publishing a correction or retraction if the article is already published.


**Post-publication changes**

Bristol University Press has a [policy on post-publication changes](https://bristoluniversitypress.co.uk/journals/author-toolkit/post-publication-changes), which includes information about publishing corrigenda, errata, expressions of concern and retractions. Requests for post-publication changes will be carefully considered and only made if necessary and in accordance with the relevant COPE guidelines.

**Appeals**

If authors believe that an editor has made an error in declining a paper, they may submit an appeal. In the appeal letter to the editor, they must state why they think the decision is mistaken. At least two editors will decide whether to invite a revised manuscript and whether re-review, or otherwise, is warranted. The editors’ decision following an appeal is final.

**Complaints**

If authors wish to make a complaint about a journal published by Bristol University Press or Policy Press they should in the first instance address this to the editor(s) of the journal, unless the editor(s) are themselves the subject of the complaint. In these circumstances the complaint may be submitted to the Journals Manager, Edwina Thorn ( [edwina.thorn@bristol.ac.uk](mailto:edwina.thorn@bristol.ac.uk)). Complaints will be investigated, discussed and escalated to members of Bristol University Press’s Senior Management Team, where appropriate, and a response will be provided.
