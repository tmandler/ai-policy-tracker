**_Economic History Review_ AI Policy**

## For Authors

_The Economic History Review_ is an academic journal that upholds traditional scholarly values. One of
the most basic of these concerns authorship. When you submit an article to the _Review_ under your name,
you are asserting that you have read the relevant literature, gathered and analysed the underpinning
data, and drawn your own conclusions. In short, you (and any co-authors) have produced the entirety
of the work yourselves. The use of Generative AI programmes, defined here as tools that use large
language models to create text, images, or other outputs (or any similar technologies), to write your
submission is strictly forbidden. The use of Generative AI programmes in the research process, for
example, data collection, coding, data cleaning, or statistical analysis is permitted and is subject to the
usual academic standards and expectations regarding accuracy, replicability and transparency.

The Economic History Society Executive Committee and the Editors of the _Economic History Review_
recognise that Generative AI can perform many useful functions to help with writing. Such programmes
may therefore be used for the following limited purposes:

• To format references consistently

• To check and correct spelling and grammar

• To improve the clarity and readability of text that expresses your own ideas, for example where
English is not your first language, provided that the words, meaning, data, analysis, and
argument remain entirely your own.

Authors are reminded that they are fully responsible for the accuracy of all aspects of the submission.

Please note that any use of Generative AI to generate or develop the substance of your argument or
analysis, or to produce text or conclusions on your behalf, is not acceptable. This policy does not restrict
appropriate scholarly uses of AI within the research process. If you are in any doubt about whether a
particular use falls within the limited purposes set out above, please contact the Editors in the first
instance before submission.

When using Generative AI to check language or improve clarity, you should confine your prompts to
the correction or clarification of text that you have already written. A prompt must not ask the tool to
introduce new evidence or argument, to develop or restructure your argument, or to substitute its own
phrasing for your analytic voice. Generative AI can produce fluent but imprecise text that falls short of
the standards of academic and social science writing, and the responsibility for ensuring that the final
wording remains your own, and remains accurate, rests entirely with the authors.

Wiley Editing Services offers expert help with English Language Editing, as well as translation,
manuscript formatting, figure illustration, figure formatting, and graphical abstract design, if required.

The Editors will use the reports of peer reviewers, AI detection software provided by the _Economic_
_History Review’s_ publisher Wiley, and their academic judgement to assess submissions. The Editors
reserve the right to reject a submission if they suspect that Generative AI or a similar technology has
been used outside of the parameters outlined above. They may also pursue further sanctions against
authors under the Economic History Society’s Code of Conduct, for example:

• to write a formal letter of censure;

• to exclude individuals, either temporarily or permanently, from specific Economic History
Society organised or supported events or activities

* * *

• to temporarily suspend membership;

• to suspend membership permanently.

This policy is designed to create transparency and to maintain the high editorial standards of the
_Economic History Review_ for both the Economic History Society and the authors who publish in it.

## For Reviewers

_The Economic History Review_ is an academic journal that upholds traditional scholarly values. One of
the most basic of these concerns authorship and the academic integrity of the peer review process. When
you agree to peer review a submission for the _Economic History Review,_ you are agreeing that you will
read the submission and write a report based on your own appraisal of the material.

In particular, you must not upload, paste, or otherwise enter any part of a submission, or any related
confidential material provided to you by the Editors, into any Generative AI programme or similar
technology in order to analyse, summarise, or otherwise assist with your review. Submissions are
confidential and entering them into such tools compromises both the confidentiality owed to authors
and the integrity of the review process.

In line with the AI Policy for Authors, such programmes may be used only to:

• Format references to the journal style

• Check and correct spelling and grammar

• Improve the clarity and readability of text that expresses your own appraisal, provided that the
words, meaning, and judgement remain entirely your own.

The Editors reserve the right to reject a review if they suspect that Generative AI or a similar technology
has been used outside of the parameters outlined above. They may also pursue further sanctions against
reviewers under the Economic History Society’s Code of Conduct, for example:

• to write a formal letter of censure;

• to exclude individuals, either temporarily or permanently, from specific Economic History
Society organised or supported events or activities

• to temporarily suspend membership;

to suspend membership permanently.

If you are in any doubt, please contact the Editors in the first instance.

## Review of this Policy

This policy will be reviewed regularly by the Economic History Society Executive Committee and the
Editors of the _Economic History Review_ to ensure that it remains current and continues to reflect
developments in Generative AI and in publishing practice.
