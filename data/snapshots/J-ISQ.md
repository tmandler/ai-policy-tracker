ISA Publishing – Guidance on AI Tools

Amanda Murdie and Brian Giblin posted on June 11, 2023 12:14

In light of recent developments in artificial intelligence (AI), the ISA Publications Committee would like to make our authors, reviewers, and readers aware of the following information. As the situation is changing rapidly, we urge the ISA community to check back for further guidance.

COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics) has released its statement on authorship, which our publisher, Oxford University Press, is following. Please see: 
https://publicationethics.org/cope-position-statements/ai-author

Detailed statements of the exact use of AI tools must be disclosed by human authors at the point of submission to any ISA journal. This information is important to ISA editors and, thus, the reviewers, as well as to readers of accepted manuscripts. Statements about the use of AI tools must be included in the cover letter upon submission. Such statements should include information on the exact AI tool and where it was used in the creation of the manuscript (for example, were AI tools used in word choice, coding, or in citation generation?). Cover letter statements should also outline rough percentages of reliance on AI tools in writing (for example, were AI tools responsible for 5% of the writing?). This information should also be in the manuscript itself; the Methods or Acknowledgements section of a manuscript will likely be the most appropriate place to include such a statement. But also note: acceptance for review and potential publication remains an editor’s discretion whether using AI tools or not.

Authors should not list AI tools in author by-lines, see 
ICMJE authorship criteria
. AI tools do not qualify as authors, because they cannot be accountable for the published work.

ISA editors are not reviewing or accepting manuscripts compiled exclusively by an AI tool at this time.

Reviewers should not use AI tools in the review process.
